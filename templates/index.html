Patient Demographics & Vitals
ℹ Patient's age in completed years. Cardiac risk generally increases after age 45 for men and 55 for women.
ℹ Biological sex at birth. Statistically, males face higher early-onset cardiac risk, while female risk rises sharply post-menopause.
ℹ Resting arterial blood pressure measured upon admission (in mm Hg). Values >130/80 mm Hg indicate hypertension.
ℹ Total serum cholesterol concentration (mg/dL). Levels >200 mg/dL contribute to plaque accumulation in coronary arteries.
ℹ Highest heart rate (bpm) achieved during exercise stress testing. Abnormally low peak rates may suggest impaired cardiac reserve.
ℹ ST segment depression measured on ECG during exercise relative to rest (in mm). Quantifies cardiac muscle oxygen starvation (ischemia).
🔬
Clinical & Diagnostic Results
ℹ Classifies chest pain: Typical Angina (effort-induced substernal discomfort), Atypical Angina, Non-Anginal Pain, or Asymptomatic.
ℹ Indicates if fasting blood glucose exceeds 120 mg/dL. Elevated blood sugar is a primary metabolic risk factor for heart disease.
ℹ Baseline ECG findings at rest: Normal, ST-T wave abnormalities (T wave inversions/ST shifts), or Left Ventricular Hypertrophy (enlarged heart).
ℹ Reports whether physical exertion triggers chest pain/angina (Yes/No), signalling inadequate coronary blood flow under workload.
🔭
Advanced Cardiac Markers
ℹ Slope of the ECG ST segment at peak exercise: Upsloping (healthy response), Flat (borderline), or Downsloping (strong indicator of myocardial ischemia).
ℹ Count of major coronary blood vessels (0 to 3) showing significant obstruction or narrowing under fluoroscopic X-ray examination.
ℹ Nuclear perfusion scan outcome: Normal blood flow, Fixed Defect (permanent scarring from prior heart attack), or Reversible Defect (active stress ischemia).
